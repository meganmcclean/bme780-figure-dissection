## What You're Doing

In Parts 1 and 2 of this assignment, you identified a figure from a paper and explained what method it illustrates and what you want to learn. Now you're doing the learning.

The goal is not to perfectly reproduce the figure. The goal is to engage with the methodology and make a real attempt to understand how it works. This could include trying to work through the methodology yourself (either with the data in the original paper or with toy data) and reflecting on what you understand, what still confuses you, and what you would need to do next to learn more. Students in this class have very different backgrounds, and what "working through the method" looks like might look different for different students. 

---

## Choose Your Format

**You may submit in either format.** Pick whichever fits how you actually work.

### Format A: The Explainer
A structured document that could serve as a tutorial or reference. Organized by topic (not by date). Should include:
- A brief description of the figure and what it claims
- An explanation of the method (the math, the biology, the experimental logic — whatever is relevant for your figure)
- Evidence that you *tried* the method yourself (code, derivations, sketches, protocol walk-throughs)
- A biological or scientific interpretation: do you believe what the figure claims?
- A reflection (what you learned, what confused you, what you will do next to learn more)
- (Optional) A worklog section (a table or list of dated entries for when and what you worked on. This doesn't need to be long)
- References and AI Usage statement (you can and should use AI to assist with understand and to assist with coding)

### Format B: The Process Documentation
A chronological document of your working process. Each entry is dated and describes what you did, what you tried, what you figured out, and what confused you. The worklog *is* the document. Should include:
- A brief framing section at the top (figure, paper, what you're trying to learn)
- Dated entries throughout the work period — include dead ends, corrections, and questions
- At least one entry that explicitly addresses: *do you believe what the figure claims?*
- A final entry with your reflection which should include what you learned, what confused you, what you need to do next to learn more.
- References and AI Usage statement (you can and should use AI to assist with understand and to assist with coding)

## What This Assignment Is Not

This assignment is NOT judging you on a polished output. This is not a term paper. The purpose of this assignment is for you to work through and better understand a method in quantitative biology using an existing published figure as a starting point. You do not need:
- An introduction/background section written for a general audience (but do include why this figure, and what the figure is trying to show)
- An extensive literature review (though do please provide references)
- Perfect prose or polished figures (but do include figures and code)
- A complete or correct analysis. It is OK to get stuck. It is OK to reduce the scope as you proceed with the assignment (for example, just understanding the first part of an analysis but getting stuck on the second half). 

A dpcument that provides evidence of a genuine attempt that didn't fully work, with an honest explanation of what went wrong and what you'd try next, is an excellent submission.

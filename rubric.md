# BME780 Figure Dissection — Grading Rubric

**Total: 100 points.** The same rubric applies to both Format A (Explainer) and Format B (Lab Notebook). Grades are based on evidence of genuine engagement, not on polish, accuracy, or completeness. A documented wrong attempt earns more credit than a polished correct answer with no explanation of how you got there.

---

## Worklog (20 points)

Evidence that work happened over time — not all at once the night before the deadline.

| Score | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| 18–20 | Entries spanning multiple weeks; each entry has real content (what you read, what you tried, what confused you, what you figured out). |
| 12–17 | Entries present but sparse, clustered in time, or thin on content. |
| 6–11  | A few entries that read more like a summary written after the fact. |
| 0–5   | No worklog, or a single entry. |

**Format A note:** The worklog can be a table at the end of the document.  
**Format B note:** The worklog *is* the document structure — dated headings count.

---

## Figure Engagement (25 points)

Evidence that you actually understand what the figure is claiming and why someone made it.

| Score | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| 22–25 | Can explain in your own words what each axis represents, what the figure is arguing, and what assumptions underlie that argument. Raises at least one substantive question or skepticism about the figure's claims. |
| 15–21 | Explains what the figure shows, but mostly paraphrases the caption without deeper engagement. |
| 8–14  | Describes the figure visually but doesn't engage with the biological or methodological claims. |
| 0–7   | Little evidence of having read or thought about the figure. |

---

## Method Attempt (35 points)

Evidence that you actually worked through the method — in whatever form is natural for your background. For computational methods: code. For mathematical methods: derivations or sketches. For experimental methods: written protocol walk-throughs or annotated diagrams.

| Score | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| 30–35 | Substantive attempt to implement, derive, or work through the method. Code runs (even partially), or derivation is worked through in detail. **Documented failures and corrections earn full credit here** — a wrong attempt with a clear explanation of what went wrong is better than a correct result with no commentary. |
| 20–29 | Partial attempt — some code or math, but significant gaps or unexplained jumps. |
| 10–19 | Minimal attempt — mostly describes what the method does rather than actually doing it. |
| 0–9   | No attempt to work through the method. |

---

## Reflection (20 points)

An honest account of what you learned, what still confuses you, and what you'd do next.

| Score | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| 18–20 | Specific and honest: names particular things that clicked, things that are still unclear, and concrete next steps. "I finally understood X when Y" or "I tried X and got confused because Z" — not generic statements. |
| 12–17 | Some reflection, but vague ("I learned a lot about PCA") or only positive. |
| 6–11  | Brief, perfunctory, or only restates what was done rather than what was learned. |
| 0–5   | No reflection. |

---

## Notes for Students

- **Background matters.** A student from a pure biology background who works through the matrix algebra by hand and gets confused but explains why is doing exactly what this assignment asks for. A student from a pure math background who explains the biological interpretation in their own words and raises questions is doing the same thing. Meet the assignment where you are.
- **Cite your resources.** If you used a textbook, a video, a blog post, or a conversation with a labmate to understand something, say so.
- **Partial credit everywhere.** There is no threshold below which you get zero — if you tried something, you get credit for trying.
